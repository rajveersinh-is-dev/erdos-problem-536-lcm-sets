# Problem Selection: Erdős Problem #536

## Why This Problem

1. **Precise statement:** The problem is well-defined and falsifiable.
2. **Significant gap:** Lower bound is $N/\log N$ times $\log\log N$, upper bound is $(221/225)N$.
3. **Mathematical depth:** The condition $[a,b]=[b,c]=[a,c]$ creates rich structure.
4. **Computational accessibility:** Can explore small cases to find patterns.
5. **Not overly famous:** Less crowded research area than major conjectures.

## Problem Classification

- **Field:** Combinatorial number theory
- **Type:** Extremal problem
- **Approach:** Combinatorial construction + structural analysis

## Research Plan

1. Explore small cases computationally to understand structure
2. Identify necessary conditions for the LCM equality
3. Develop new constructions or upper bound techniques
4. Attempt to prove $f(N) = o(N)$ or improve bounds
